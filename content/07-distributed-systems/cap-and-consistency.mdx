# CAP and Consistency

> **Difficulty:** Beginner → production engineering. **Goal:** Develop the intuition, understand implementation choices, and explain tradeoffs in interviews.

## 1. Why this matters

Distributed nodes cannot avoid network partitions, so consistency and availability tradeoffs matter during partitions.

## 2. Mental model

CAP concerns how a partitioned system handles consistent responses versus availability. Outside failures, latency and consistency tradeoffs still exist; consistency is not a single Boolean.

## 3. Explore the concept

Sketch a small system with two clients and three backend nodes. Change one assumption (load, latency, or a server failure), then trace how the behavior changes. Explain what should remain correct and which metric will get worse.

## 4. Java / Spring Boot deep dive

Use transactions where guarantees are needed; document exactly what a Spring Boot endpoint promises after a write, including read-after-write behavior.

## 5. Production tradeoffs

Eventual consistency requires user-facing expectations and reconciliation. CAP does not claim you may simply pick any two properties at all times.

## 6. Check your understanding

**Question:** What does CAP force you to think about?

**Answer:** How the system responds when nodes cannot communicate yet clients continue issuing requests.

## 7. Try it in a project

Build a small Spring Boot service that demonstrates this principle. Record a baseline (latency, throughput, correctness), change one variable, and compare the results. Document what your measurements actually prove rather than assuming a benchmark result.
